# Invalidated and superseded evidence

Nothing here is deleted. Each entry records a number that was published or
generated and the exact reason it cannot be used as qualification evidence.

## `superseded-receipt-0fb93df0.json`

Generated by `npm run verify:integrity-receipts` and committed at `bf685663`.

Superseded, for three independent reasons:

- **Not generated at `ba3fb9f1`.** It records `repository_revision`
  `0fb93df0`, two commits before the head whose performance the checkpoint
  described. A receipt is evidence about the revision it measured, not about a
  later one.
- **Baseline not supplied by the required command.** The comparison it carries
  was produced by pointing `--baseline` at a checkout prepared by hand outside
  the command. Nobody could reproduce it from a clean tree, which is the
  property that makes a receipt evidence rather than an assertion.
- **Inventory checksum stale after corpus growth.** The remediation added
  source and test files after the receipt was generated, so its inventory
  checksum no longer describes the corpus the checkpoint's numbers were
  attributed to.

## The `0.993x` / `1.070x` claim

Published in the `ba3fb9f1` checkpoint as the focused performance of
`dccf00cf → ba3fb9f1`.

An independent manual comparison of the same two heads measured `0.988x` and
`0.994x`.

Both pairs are within budget and neither is alarming, but they are not the same
measurement, and the published pair was not reproducible through the required
command. `receipt-dccf00cf-vs-ba3fb9f1.json` in this directory supersedes the
published pair with a run that is.

The corrected numbers are whatever that receipt records. No pair was selected
for being favourable: the runner emits raw samples, both counterbalanced
session orders, and every invalidated run, so the arithmetic can be checked
rather than trusted.

## Why measurement changed

The earlier runner let each arm extract its own input and then compared the
build times. That measures two extractions as though they were one. The runner
now extracts once under a declared authority, hands byte-identical canonical
input to both arms, verifies the two arms received the same input checksum, and
discards any session where they did not.

Arms also now run in separate processes with counterbalanced order. Sharing a
process let the first arm warm the JIT for the second, which moves a ratio for
reasons that have nothing to do with either head.
